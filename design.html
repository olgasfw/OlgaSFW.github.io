The editorial
product atlas.

Confident, precise, spacious, and quietly expressive. A portfolio that treats each project as an object worth examining.

Visual direction derived from your six book-reference screenshots.

Why this direction.

The reference spreads use oversized light sans-serif headings, generous negative space, large object photography, narrow information columns, and fine rules. That creates authority through curation and hierarchy.

Here, product interfaces take the place of ceramic objects. Project facts act like museum captions; the case study carries the deeper story.

Translate the principles.

Keep: dramatic scale, an asymmetric grid, precise captions, white backgrounds, and simple typography.

Adapt: use comfortable body text and short readable passages, rather than reproducing the book’s dense columns. Keep navigation visible and the next action clear.

Color with a purpose.

Paper / #FFFFFF
Ink / #20201E
Object field / #E9E7DF
Cover yellow / #FFC400

White and ink establish clarity. A warm neutral frames the work. Yellow, taken from the cover’s visual character, marks occasional chapter breaks and calls to action.

Desktop → mobile.

Desktop uses broad two-part spreads with imagery beside the narrative. Mobile stacks the title, image, facts, and story in reading order.

At 700px and below, navigation becomes an expandable menu, columns become a single reading stream, and headings scale down without losing their presence.

Use your browser’s responsive mode to compare 1440px desktop and 390px mobile. All six pages share the same responsive system.

Before a public launch: confirm contact details and biography, replace illustrative screens with your actual work, and complete evidence-based project narratives.
